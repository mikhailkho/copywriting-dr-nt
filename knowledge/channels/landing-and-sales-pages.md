# Channel: Landing Pages & Long-Form Sales Pages

Sources: Bly Ch. 11 (microsite, short landing page, transaction page, Waterfront model, critiques), Kennedy (sales letter system), Masterson/Forde (leads), Schwartz, Whitman (checklist, guarantees, coupons), Caples (inquiry boosters).

## 1. Choose the page type
| Type | When | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **Transaction page** | Most-aware; known product | Minimal copy, clear form |
| **Short landing page** | Lead capture, demo, free report, familiar product | One screen + a few paragraphs |
| **Long-form sales page (microsite)** | Considered purchase, unaware/solution-aware, higher price, direct sale | 4–8 "letter pages" equivalent, one continuous scroll |
| **VSL / webinar registration + replay page** | High-ticket or complex | Video-led + supporting copy |
| **Product page (ecommerce)** | See `product-pages-and-ecommerce.md` | |

## 2. First screen (above the fold) must do
1. **Select the audience** ("For ___:" flag).
2. **State the Primary Promise** (specific, true).
3. **Show the mechanism or proof hint** (a number, name, demo, rating).
4. **One clear primary action** (button text that says what happens: "Get the checklist", "See pricing", "Book a 15-minute audit").
5. **Match the ad/email that sent them** (message match = same promise, same words, same visual cue).
Mobile first: the first screen must work on a phone without scrolling gymnastics.

## 3. Long-page skeleton
1. Headline + subhead (+ byline/credibility line if relevant)
2. Lead (archetype per diagnosis; tell a story if indirect)
3. Desire scenes
4. Problem recognition / false belief handling
5. Mechanism: "why this works"
6. Proof stack (case numbers, testimonials, logos, demos, data)
7. Who it's for / not for (damaging admission)
8. Offer stack (what you get, value, price, payment plans)
9. Guarantee (prominent)
10. Objection FAQ (answer top 5–8; include the awkward ones)
11. Close: summary + CTA + deadline (true)
12. P.S. + repeat CTA
Repeat the CTA at natural decision points (after proof, after offer, at the end).

## 4. Page-level conversion levers (hypotheses to test)
- Offer terms and guarantee prominence
- Headline idea and lead type
- Proof placement (proof near the claim)
- Social proof specificity (names, photos, results)
- Form friction (fields, steps)
- Load speed and mobile layout
- Price anchoring/payment plans
- Reading flow: scannable subheads, bullets, captions
- Video vs text (test)
- Exit offers (honest)

## 5. Design principles that serve copy
Editorial look; high contrast; large readable type; short lines; generous white space; relevant imagery with captions; faces looking at the viewer when natural; restrained animation; avoid sticky pop-ups that block reading on mobile; clear hierarchy (Halbert's "layout shouldn't be noticed"). Accessibility: contrast ratios, alt text, keyboard navigation, readable fonts.

## 6. Conversion plumbing
Tracking (UTMs, pixel/server events), consent management, GDPR/PDPA notices where relevant, thank-you page that delivers the promise and the next step, autoresponder confirmation, error handling, accessible forms.

## 7. Compliance on pages
Claims substantiated; earnings/health/finance disclaimers specific, not buried; testimonials: typical-results context, material connections disclosed; price and recurring billing visible before the final click; cancellation instructions clear; clear identification as advertising when needed; privacy policy and terms.

## 8. Critique checklist (quick)
- Could a stranger restate what's on offer in 5 seconds?
- Does the headline match the ad/email promise?
- Is there one primary action?
- Is every claim followed by proof within a screen or two?
- Do top objections have answers on the page?
- Is the guarantee easy to find and believe?
- Are deadlines and scarcity real and logged?
- Would the page survive skimming (impulsive path)?

## 9. AI notes
Have Claude produce: (a) page outline; (b) 5 headline ideas by lead type; (c) FAQ from real objections; (d) microcopy for buttons/forms; (e) a message-match table (ad → page). Do not let it invent statistics, customer logos or reviews.
