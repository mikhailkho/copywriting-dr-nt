# Channel: Email (Lifecycle & Campaign)

Sources: Bly Ch. 12 (15 tips, deliverability), Brunson (Soap Opera, Seinfeld, house list), Halbert (A-pile/B-pile, personal intimacy and immediacy, reply mechanics), Kennedy (sequences, P.S., teaser), Caples (offers), Hopkins (letters: follow along the original interest).

## 1. Principles
- **Permission and relationship first.** Email works best to people who already know you (Bly). Respect consent, unsubscribes and privacy law in the audience's jurisdiction.
- **Sender name, subject line and preheader are the "envelope".** Make the email look like it belongs in the personal pile without deceiving anyone.
- **One purpose, one action** per email. A sequence has several emails with distinct angles.
- **Front-load:** the first screen carries a mini-version of the whole message (Bly); the rest elaborates for those who need it.
- **Tone:** helpful, specific, human. Information is currency; hype lowers trust.

## 2. Email anatomy
| Element | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **From** | Person (best for relationships), brand (well-known), team; consistent |
| **Subject** | 4 U's; curiosity with a payoff; avoid false urgency/deceptive prefixes; test length |
| **Preheader** | Extend, not repeat |
| **Opening line** | Benefit/curiosity; the "lead" |
| **Body** | One idea; story/proof; short paragraphs; scannable; first two links get most clicks |
| **CTA** | Explicit; button + text link; set expectations of what happens next |
| **P.S.** | Restate deadline or reward (read by skimmers) |
| **Footer** | Identity, address where legally required, unsubscribe |

## 3. Sequences — templates

**Welcome / bonding (5 emails)** (Soap Opera)
1. Set the stage & deliver the promised asset
2. Drama/backstory — the problem and the wall
3. Epiphany — the insight
4. Hidden benefits — stories
5. CTA with true urgency (e.g., bonus window)

**Offer sequence (4–6 emails)**
1. Announce offer + core promise
2. Case story/proof
3. Objection email (top objection answered)
4. Bonus/guarantee
5. Deadline reminder (real)
6. Last call + P.S.

**Ongoing nurture (weekly or segment-based)**
Story or lesson + soft bridge. Rotate: customer story, myth-busting, behind-the-scenes, how-to, offer. Avoid selling every time.

**Re-engagement / sunset**
Check-in → value or incentive → "should we keep emailing?" → remove inactive subscribers (protects deliverability).

## 4. Deliverability & hygiene (principles)
Permission-based lists; double opt-in where appropriate; consistent sending identity; authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC); clean lists; monitor bounces/complaints; avoid spam trigger patterns and invalid HTML; plain-text alternative; moderate image-to-text ratio; link domains consistent. (Specific filter rules change; verify current provider guidance.)

## 5. Frequency
Choose cadence by audience expectation, content supply and economics. Start with a sequence, then test segmented frequency. The "daily" model (Brunson) works for some entertainment-oriented personalities; many businesses can't sustain quality daily. Measure unsubscribes, complaints, and revenue per subscriber, not opens alone (apple privacy features distort opens).

## 6. Personal/outbound B2B email
Short; one clear reason for contacting this person; specific relevance; low-friction ask; no pretend "Re:"; compliance with local anti-spam law and cold-outreach rules (identity, opt-out line, lawful recipient sourcing). When the sender is unknown and has little proof, read `b2b-and-service-businesses.md` §3a first: candour and clarity beat a clever hook. See also that file for the general B2B rules.

## 7. Metrics
Delivery, inbox placement (seed tests), click-through, conversion, revenue per recipient, unsubscribes, spam complaints, list growth/churn, sequence payback. Use holdouts for incrementality when possible.

## 8. Compliance snapshot (verify current rules; not legal advice)
- **Consent & identity:** clear sender identification; functioning unsubscribe; honour opt-outs promptly. Examples of regimes: CAN-SPAM (US), GDPR/ePrivacy (EU/UK), CASL (Canada), Singapore's Spam Control Act and PDPA (consent and Do-Not-Call rules for messages).
- **Content:** no deceptive headers or subjects; substantiated claims; clear disclosures for promotions and endorsements.

## 9. AI notes
- Provide the sender persona, audience stage, offer terms and proof; ask for 3 subject-line angles per email with the 4 U's rationale.
- Generate sequences as **arcs**, not independent emails; keep a "story bank" with real stories only.
- Have Claude check for repeated phrases across the sequence (sameness).
