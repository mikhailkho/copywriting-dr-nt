# Compliance & Ethics Gate

> **Not legal advice.** This is a practical gate to catch common problems before publishing. Rules vary by country, sector and platform and change over time. When stakes are high (health, finance, income, legal, children, regulated industries), have a qualified professional review, and verify the current rule text.

Several of the source books are comfortable with tactics (fear appeals, "false logic," intimidation, agitation, scarcity) that are now regulated or reputationally risky. This skill keeps the psychology but applies this gate.

## 1. The three-part test
1. **True:** Is each claim, number, quote, story and scarcity statement true and substantiated *now*?
2. **Fair:** Would a reasonable person in the target audience be misled by the overall impression (not only the literal words)?
3. **Respectful:** Does it respect autonomy and vulnerability, with easy opt-out and clear terms?

If any answer is no or unknown → fix or remove.

## 2. Claim types and what to hold

| Claim type | Hold on file | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Performance / result** ("lose 10 lb", "double sales") | Study, test data, representative case data; typical-results statement | Avoid "up to" cherry-picking; show typical |
| **Comparative** ("faster than X") | Head-to-head evidence, fair basis | Avoid disparaging unsupported comparisons |
| **Price / discount** ("50% off") | Evidence of prior price over a reasonable period | No fake "regular price" |
| **Scarcity / urgency** | Inventory/capacity/date logs | Timers must be real |
| **Testimonials / reviews** | Written consent, authenticity, typical-results context | Disclose incentives/material connections; no fake reviews |
| **Endorsements / influencers** | Contract + disclosure | Clear, conspicuous disclosure format |
| **Health / medical** | Competent scientific evidence; regulator rules | Avoid cure/prevent/treat claims unless authorised |
| **Financial / investment / income** | Regulator-approved statements, risk warnings, income disclosures | No guarantees; include risk warnings |
| **Environmental / sustainability** | Evidence, specific scope | Avoid vague "eco-friendly" |
| **Guarantees** | Policy and operations to honour | State conditions plainly |
| **Awards / "#1" / "best"** | Source, date, scope | Avoid unqualified superlatives |
| **Origin / "Made in"** | Supply chain evidence | Follow local rules |

## 3. Techniques from the books — status in this skill

| Technique | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Problem–agitation–solution | Kennedy | Allowed; proportion and truth required; avoid shaming |
| Fear appeal | Whitman | Allowed only with the four conditions and a genuine solution |
| "False logic" framing | Bly/Masterson | **Not allowed** where it creates a false impression |
| Intimidation / taking a position | Kennedy | Allowed if the qualification/limit is real |
| Scarcity/deadline | Many | Allowed if real; else prohibited |
| Fake "special circumstance" reasons | Halbert (misread) | Prohibited unless true |
| Hiding the order card / costs | Halbert | Hide the *pitch timing*, never *terms or price* |
| Pre-checked boxes / buried opt-ins | Bly's example | Prohibited |
| Free-plus-shipping with continuity | Brunson | Allowed with prominent, upfront disclosure and easy cancellation |
| Disguised ads (advertorial, "reporter" format) | Schwartz/Kennedy | Must be clearly labelled as advertising where required |
| Celebrity/authority "transfer" | Whitman | Only with real, permitted association |
| Fabricated mechanisms/secrets | (risk in Brown/Masterson) | Prohibited |
| "Quiet" negative option / hard-to-cancel subscriptions | (various) | Prohibited |

## 4. Jurisdiction pointers (starting points to verify)
- **Singapore:** Singapore Code of Advertising Practice (SCAP) and the Advertising Standards Authority of Singapore (ASAS); Consumer Protection (Fair Trading) Act (unfair practices; enforced by CCCS); Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA) including Do Not Call and consent rules; Spam Control Act; sector rules (healthcare advertising, MAS-regulated financial advertising, legal profession rules).
- **United States:** FTC Act (deception/unfairness), FTC Endorsement Guides, substantiation doctrine, CAN-SPAM, telemarketing rules, negative-option/subscription rules, state laws; sector rules (FDA, SEC/FINRA).
- **United Kingdom:** CAP/BCAP Codes (ASA), DMCC/Consumer Protection Regs, PECR/GDPR.
- **European Union:** Unfair Commercial Practices Directive, GDPR/ePrivacy, sector rules.
- **Platforms:** Google/Meta/TikTok/LinkedIn ad policies (health, finance, employment, housing, personal attributes, before/after, misleading claims), marketplace policies.
These are signposts only. Check current text and local counsel.

## 5. Pre-flight questions (ask the user/client)
- Which jurisdictions and platforms will this run in?
- Are we a regulated business or selling a regulated product?
- What evidence do we hold for each claim?
- Do we have consent for names, images, testimonials?
- Are subscription/trial terms and cancellation process documented and visible?
- What happens after purchase (fulfilment, refunds)?
- Who signs off legally?

## 6. Ethical guardrails for AI-assisted copy
- No generation of fake testimonials, reviews, experts, case studies or social proof.
- No invented statistics or sources. Unknown stays unknown.
- Flag emotional pressure; require human approval for fear/guilt/shame-based appeals.
- Avoid targeting vulnerable people with exploitative pitches (debt, grief, serious illness, children).
- Don't impersonate real people or organisations; don't mimic a real person's voice or brand without authority.
- Respect copyright: do not reproduce copyrighted ads, books or sales letters; use principles and original expression.
- Be transparent where required about AI use or generated media.

## 7. Substantiation file (create for each campaign)
```
CLAIM → EVIDENCE (link/document) → DATE → OWNER → EXPIRY/REVIEW DATE → APPROVAL
```
Store with the draft; renew on expiry.

## 8. Red flags — stop and escalate
- Income/health/finance outcome promises without strong evidence
- Fake scarcity/countdown or fake "reasons why"
- Testimonials from people who didn't say them
- Hidden fees/recurring charges
- Pressure aimed at vulnerable audiences
- "Secret" or "cure" language implying suppressed knowledge
- Copy that depends on the reader misunderstanding something
