# Story, Identity and Belief

Sources: Brunson (*Expert Secrets*: Epiphany Bridge, Hero's Two Journeys, False Belief Patterns, 3 Secrets; *DotCom Secrets*: Attractive Character, Soap Opera/Seinfeld), Masterson/Forde (Story/Secret/Proclamation leads), Caples (story headlines), Schwartz (identification, gradualization), Whitman (ego morphing, mental movies, inoculation, belief re-ranking), Kennedy (case histories, damaging admission), Halbert (specific, relevant detail).

**Principle:** Stories are how belief changes. Facts argue; stories let the reader *experience* the shift. But in the NT canon, stories must be **true, specific and consented**.

## 1. When to use story
- Reader is unaware/problem-aware and resists direct claims (Story/Proclamation lead).
- Belief barriers exist ("this won't work for me").
- You need identification (reader wants to be like the protagonist).
- Long-form sales pages, webinars, founder pages, email sequences, video ads, testimonial case studies.

## 2. Story types
| Type | Purpose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Origin / Epiphany Bridge** | Show the journey to the insight | Needs a real backstory and real "wall" |
| **Customer case** | Prove results for people like the reader | Specifics: starting point, action, numbers, timeline, quote |
| **Parable** | Teach a principle in a short anecdote | Keep factual or label as illustration |
| **Testimonial narrative** | Third-party belief | Use their words; verify; consent; typical-results disclosures |
| **Skeptic's conversion** | "I thought it was a scam until…" | Only if the real person said it |
| **Historic/analogy** | Rail vs fiber optics, astronaut nutrient | Analogies must be accurate |
| **Day-in-the-life / future scene** | Desire intensification | Hypothetical scenes labelled as such where claims could mislead |

## 3. The Epiphany Bridge (8 sections; Brunson)
1. **Backstory** (start where the listener is; give vested interest)
2. **Desires** (external goal + internal struggle)
3. **The Wall** (the problem you hit with the old approach)
4. **The Epiphany** (the new insight/opportunity)
5. **The Plan** (what you did)
6. **The Conflict** (obstacles)
7. **The Achievement** (the result)
8. **The Transformation** (who you became / what changed)
Use it for a founder story, a client case study, a webinar section, a long email.

## 4. Hero's two journeys
Every strong story has an **external journey** (what happened) and an **internal journey** (what the protagonist believed and how it changed). The audience should find themselves in the internal one.

## 5. False belief mapping (Brunson)
For the idea/offer, list 10–20 false beliefs; for each:
1. The belief ("If I lose weight I'll be miserable").
2. The experience that created it.
3. The story they now tell.
4. The replacement story/evidence (yours or a customer's).
Classify as **Vehicle** (is this the right method?), **Internal** (can I do it?), **External** (will money/time/other people/outside forces stop me?). Make one proof/story per belief.

## 6. Attractive Character (persona)
Elements: **backstory**, **parables**, **flaws**, **polarity** (take positions). Identities: Leader, Adventurer/Crusader, Reporter/Evangelist, Reluctant Hero. Keep tone consistent over time (Hopkins). **NT additions:** the persona is a real person's authentic voice, or a brand voice that doesn't claim a personal history it lacks. Never create a fictional "founder story" or fake expert.

## 7. Story leads — craft rules (Masterson/Forde, Caples)
- One story, one idea, one emotion; the promise must be embedded.
- Start in medias res or at the moment of tension ("They laughed when I sat down at the piano…").
- Be brief; tie back to the product by the end of the lead.
- Use concrete, sensory detail (names, places, numbers, objects).
- Don't over-explain the moral; let the reader draw it.

## 8. Dramatic email sequences
- **Soap Opera Sequence (5 emails):** set the stage → high drama/backstory → epiphany → hidden benefits → urgency/CTA. Each ends with an open loop.
- **Seinfeld emails:** short stories about everyday events with a bridge to the offer.
- **Open-loop rules:** the loop must close; don't bait-and-switch.

## 9. Proof inside story
Pin numbers, dates and verifiable details; show the *process*; include a mild setback to be credible; avoid perfect hero arcs; attribute quotes; add typical-results context where required.

## 10. Ethics gate for story
- Real people, real events, real results. If composite, label it ("Composite example").
- Consent for names/images/results; keep records.
- No fabricated "I used to be broke" backstories for personas.
- AI must never generate testimonials, case studies or founder histories. It may structure and edit **real material the user supplies**.

## 11. AI workflow for story
1. Interview the human (the model asks Epiphany Bridge questions; the user answers).
2. Claude drafts the narrative from *verbatim* answers; it flags missing details instead of inventing them.
3. The user verifies every fact and adds specifics (numbers, dates).
4. Edit for voice and rhythm (anti-slop pass) without altering facts.
5. Store the story and its proof in a "story bank" for reuse.
