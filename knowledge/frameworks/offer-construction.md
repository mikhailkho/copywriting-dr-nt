# Offer Construction — the proposition, the stack, the risk reversal

Sources: Halbert Ch. 21 (propositions, reason-why, "you are special"), Kennedy Steps 7, 13 (price minimisers, guarantees, premiums, deadlines, sequences), Whitman Ch. 4 (value, buying ease, coupons), Caples Ch. 13 (32 ways to get inquiries), Masterson/Forde Ch. 4 (Offer Lead formula), Brunson (value ladder, stack, free-plus-shipping), Hopkins Ch. 3, 13 (service, samples), Bly (USP, secondary promise).

> "Sell people what they want to buy." — Halbert. Then give them an offer so clear and safe that saying yes is the easy path.

## 1. Offer anatomy

| Element | Question | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Core promise** | What result for whom? | From the Big Idea Card |
| **Deliverable(s)** | Exactly what do they receive? | Specifics: quantity, format, timing |
| **Price & terms** | How much, how paid, recurring? | Total cost clear; no hidden recurrence |
| **Value framing** | Why is the price a fair or great trade? | Compare apples to oranges (honestly), ROI, cost of the problem, cost of alternatives, amortise |
| **Bonuses / premiums** | What extra reduces resistance? | Relevant, tangible, valued; test double premiums |
| **Guarantee / risk reversal** | What if it doesn't work? | Stronger than competitors', redundant statement, specific conditions |
| **Reason why** | Why this deal, why now? | Must be true |
| **Scarcity / deadline** | Is there a real limit? | Stock, capacity, event date, bonus availability |
| **Ease of action** | How easy is it to say yes? | One path, minimal fields, all payment options, human help |
| **Next step** | What exactly do they do? | One call to action |

## 2. Proposition patterns (Halbert/Kennedy/Whitman/Caples)
- Straight swap (X dollars for X product); buy one get one; quantity discount; early-bird/late penalty; trial; pay later ("send no money; pay in a week"); club/continuity; introductory half-price; free sample/booklet/report; premium for fast response; trade-in; gift with purchase.
- **Reason-why library (true only):** inventory clearance, overstock, seasonal reset, anniversary, sample photography stock, launch pricing for first N customers, beta pricing for testimonials, capacity limit for service quality, bundle logic.
- **"Because you are special" (Halbert):** a deal tied to something genuinely true about the recipient (profession, location, surname, past purchase, referral). Needs real data; otherwise it is false personalisation.
- **Invitation offers** (Masterson/Forde): for genuinely exclusive products, applications, memberships, events.

## 3. Price framing (honest versions)
- **Compare to a different, relevant alternative** (seminar + travel vs recording; cost of an hour of lost production; employee time) — not to a fictional "regular price."
- **Make bulk visible:** number of items/steps/pages, weight, hours of content; itemise the parts so the sum shows (stack).
- **Development cost story:** what it took to produce (research, testing, experts) — if true.
- **Amortise:** per day/week/use, only when the total price is also clearly shown.
- **ROI:** "A $199 course that generated $1,000 in extra income is 5x" requires evidence; use real ranges and note typical vs best results.
- **Anchoring:** state the highest-value alternative first (the done-for-you option) only if it genuinely exists and some buy it.
- **Instalments:** show total cost and term; "3 payments of $X" is acceptable if disclosed clearly.
- **Don't discount against a fake list price.** Reference prices must be real, recent and substantiated.

## 4. Guarantees (Kennedy)
Types: basic money-back; refund-and-keep-the-premium; redundant wording ("every penny, no questions"); free trial with a long window; guarantee as the headline; outcome guarantees ("lose X or Y"); *guarantee the reading* (pay if the reader feels their time was wasted). Rules: only promise what you will honour; spell out conditions simply; service-level guarantees (time, response) are strong differentiators; a long, strong guarantee is the cheapest conversion lever (Whitman: "longer than your competitor's").

## 5. Premium/bonus design
- Relevant to the core result; a "dissolving bonus" removes the last objection.
- Name it and value it honestly (cost of replacement or what similar sells for).
- Make a bonus time- or quantity-limited only if it truly is.
- Test one vs two premiums; Kennedy saw average order size rise when a second premium was tied to a larger order.

## 6. Deadlines & scarcity — the honest implementation
- Real end dates with a logged reason (event, cohort start, price rise date, stock level).
- Evergreen funnels: personalised deadlines are acceptable only if the deadline is actually enforced after it passes.
- Never reset countdown timers or show fake "only 3 left."
- "Limited number available" must reflect real inventory/capacity.

## 7. Ease of action (Whitman: "make it easy, then ask")
All contact routes; all common payment options; instalments; "bill me later" where viable; clear directions for local; "It's easy to order/schedule/quote" statements; confirmation expectations; short forms; accessible mobile flow; a human help line. Remove every unnecessary click, field and choice.

## 8. Value ladder & sequence (Brunson/Kennedy)
- **Ladder:** irresistible, low-risk entry offer → core product → premium → recurring. Each rung gives real value so ascension is natural. Missing rungs mean wasted customer value ("a five-thousand-dollar buyer lead — sell them something else").
- **Sequence:** 2–5 touches over days/weeks rather than a one-shot; each touch has a distinct angle (offer → bonus/guarantee → social proof → last chance with a real deadline). Kennedy: letters 2–3 combined often double the response of the first.

## 9. Offer lead formula (Masterson/Forde)
1. Lead with the most emotionally compelling detail of the deal. 2. Underscore the most valuable benefit. 3. Elaborate that benefit in the lead. 4. Give a reason why. If there's a catch, say so early and show why it's a net positive.

## 10. Offer testing priorities
Order of leverage (typical): **offer structure > price/terms > guarantee > bonus > headline/lead > body > layout.** Test one change at a time; keep a control.

## 11. Pre-launch offer checklist
- [ ] A skeptical customer can restate the deal in one sentence.
- [ ] Total price, billing frequency and cancellation steps are visible before purchase.
- [ ] Every scarcity/deadline claim has a true logged reason.
- [ ] The guarantee is honoured by operations (refund process exists).
- [ ] Bonuses exist and deliver on time.
- [ ] Reference prices are real and recent.
- [ ] Legal review done where required (health, finance, income claims, subscriptions).
