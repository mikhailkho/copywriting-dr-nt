# Awareness × Sophistication — the diagnostic that decides everything else

Sources: Schwartz *Breakthrough Advertising* (original), Masterson & Forde *Great Leads* (awareness → lead type), Todd Brown (Promise Exposure Spectrum, Prospect Awareness Pyramid), Whitman (Transtheoretical Model), Kennedy (readership paths).

**Use:** answer both questions *before* writing a headline, lead, email subject or ad hook. State the answers in the deliverable and list the evidence.

## A. Awareness: what does the reader already know?

| Stage (Schwartz / modern label) | They know… | Lead must… | Typical best lead types | Typical length |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Most aware** | You, the product, want it | Give the deal: price, bonus, deadline, guarantee, availability | Offer lead; Invitation | Short |
| **2. Product aware** | The product, not sure it's right | Prove it; answer "why this, why now"; add new proof, mechanism or limit-remover | Promise lead; proof/testimonial lead; comparison | Medium |
| **3. Solution aware** | The result they want; not that you provide it | Name the desired result, prove it's possible, show your product as the mechanism | Promise; Big Secret; Story | Medium–long |
| **4. Problem aware** | The problem; not the solutions | Name the problem in their words, dramatise (only as far as is truthful), present the solution as inevitable | Problem–Solution; Story; Question | Long |
| **5. Unaware** | Nothing about the problem or you; maybe general unease | Identify with their state of mind / world; make them curious or newsworthy-informed; sell only the next sentence | Proclamation; Story; Big Secret (indirect) | Long (needs a payoff-rich first screen) |

**Evidence sources to place an audience:** list type (house list vs cold), search terms and queries used, ad-comment language, survey/review text, sales-call objections, whether a category name exists in their vocabulary, how many competitors address them directly.

**Caveat — "too aware":** jaded markets can be highly aware yet cynical. A plain offer fails there; go indirect to rebuild trust.

**Segment rule:** one asset serves one dominant stage. If you must serve several, layer: headline for the dominant stage; subhead and first paragraph handle adjacent stages; deep content is skimmable.

## B. Sophistication: how many claims have they already heard?

Sample 10–20 competitor ads/pages/emails and the market's common phrases.

| Stage | Market state | What works | Typical error |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | First claim in market | State the promise simply and directly; dramatise | Over-complicating |
| **2** | Claim is known and still believed | Enlarge the claim (more, faster, easier), push to the edge of believability, add proof/guarantee | Exaggeration beyond proof |
| **3** | Claims exhausted, skepticism rising | **New mechanism** — how it works that is new to them | Repeating the old promise |
| **4** | Competitors copied the mechanism | Elaborate the mechanism (easier, faster, surer, fewer limits) or find a new one | Staying generic |
| **5** | Market numb to claims and mechanisms | **Identification** — sell identity, belonging, shared experience, honest story; or a new category/"new opportunity" | More claims |

Brown's PES maps to the same ladder: Promise → Promise Expanded → Promise + Unique Mechanism → Promise + Expanded Mechanism → Prospect Experience.

## C. Putting them together

| | Low awareness | High awareness |
|---|---|---|
| **Low sophistication (new market)** | Simple promise + dramatised problem; teach the category | Simple offer; speed matters |
| **High sophistication** | Identification/story/new angle (Big Idea) that feels like news | Mechanism + proof + risk reversal; make the deal unusually clear |

**Rules of thumb**
- High awareness + low sophistication → direct, short, offer-led.
- Low awareness + high sophistication → hardest case; needs a Big Idea (see `big-idea-and-mechanism.md`).
- A new product in an old category is a sophistication problem (new mechanism). An old product in a new market is an awareness problem (teach, name the desire).

## D. Transtheoretical Model (Whitman) as a cross-check
Precontemplation → Contemplation → Preparation → Action → Maintenance. Use for lifecycle messaging: acquisition content for stages 1–3, conversion for 3–4, retention/referral for 5.

## E. Output template (include in every copy deliverable)

```
AUDIENCE: <who, in their words>
AWARENESS STAGE: <1–5 + evidence>
SOPHISTICATION STAGE: <1–5 + competitor claims sampled>
DOMINANT EMOTION / MASS DESIRE: <one>
LEAD ARCHETYPE: <offer / promise / problem-solution / secret / proclamation / story> + why
LENGTH LOGIC: <emotion × involvement × price × ask size>
```

## F. AI-era notes
- Models default to stage 1–2 phrasing ("Unlock more X, faster"). Force the diagnosis, then require a variant per candidate stage if uncertain, and let tests decide.
- Re-diagnose when a competitor launches a new mechanism or the market's language shifts; ideas expire.
