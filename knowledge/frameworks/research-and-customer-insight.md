# Research & Customer Insight — the work before the words

Sources: Hopkins Ch. 11 (information), Halbert Ch. 5–10, 15, 22 (student of markets, nugget notes, swipe file), Kennedy Step 1–3 (10 market questions, index cards, damaging admission), Bly Ch. 4–5 (BFD, 22 motivators, sources key-coded), Whitman (LF8), Masterson/Forde (core emotions via listening), Brown (specific + general input, combinations), Brunson (Secret Formula).

The raw material for good copy is in your market, your prospects and your product, not in your own head (the idea attributed to Schwartz, via Brown).

## 1. What you must know before drafting (minimum viable research)

| Area | Questions | Typical sources |
|---|---|---|
| **Market (hungry crowd)** | Who buys this already? What else do they buy, how recently and for how much? Is the desire growing or fading? | Customer data, category sales, ad libraries, search trends, competitor activity, marketplaces |
| **Customer** | Kennedy's 10: what keeps them awake; what they fear; who/what they're angry at; top three daily frustrations; coming trends; secret desires; decision biases; own language; who else sells to them; how others failed | Support tickets, sales-call recordings, reviews (yours and competitors'), forums, Reddit/FB groups, interview transcripts |
| **BFD (Bly/Masterson)** | **B**eliefs, **F**eelings, **D**esires — in narrative form | Same as above; then write it out and agree it with the team |
| **Language** | Exact phrases and metaphors they use for the problem and result | Verbatim quotes in reviews/calls |
| **Product** | Features → benefits → hidden benefit; true mechanism; process steps others skip; numbers; limits; honest flaws | Use the product; talk to makers; lab/test data; process walk-through |
| **Proof** | Case results, testimonials with permission, data, third-party validation, guarantees we can honour | Customer success records, public data, certifications |
| **Competition** | Claims, mechanisms, offers, price points, guarantees, style | Competitor ads/pages/emails; swipe file |
| **Objections** | Reasons not to respond (one card each) | Sales objections, abandoned-cart reasons, lost-deal notes |
| **Constraints** | Legal/compliance, brand voice, channel specs, budget, timelines | Client brief |

## 2. Techniques that carry over unchanged
- **Observe, don't ask:** what people *do* buy outranks what they say. Use survey data skeptically (Halbert's beer, tabloid, plays-vs-movies stories; Caples's opinion-test biases).
- **Hot-list hierarchy → modern audience quality:** recency, frequency, monetary value, similarity, own customers first. Translate to first-party data, past purchasers, high-intent search/ad audiences.
- **Watch what repeats:** ads, mailings and pages that run for months have hit a nerve. Study the repeated ones, not the new experiments.
- **Emotional turning points:** new baby, new job, bankruptcy, move, marriage. Time offers to life events where legitimate and consented.
- **Seven-times rule (Caples via Brown):** gather far more information than you'll use.
- **Nugget notes (Halbert):** read everything; jot fragments without judging; star the best (*, **, ***); step away 1–2 days to incubate; the big idea often arrives afterwards.
- **Index cards (Kennedy):** one card per feature/benefit; one per reason not to respond; sort by importance.
- **Source key-coding (Bly):** tag every fact with its source in the draft; strip after verification.
- **Specific + general input (Brown):** domain facts *and* current events/history/culture to find connections ("railroad → fiber optics").
- **Hidden benefit (Nicholas via Kennedy):** the non-obvious profound benefit (recruiting system → more golf).
- **Under-told process (Hopkins):** the honest steps everyone performs but nobody advertises.

## 3. Desire mapping (choose one primary)
Rate candidate desires on **urgency, staying power, scope** (Schwartz). Use LF8 and learned wants (Whitman) plus Bly's 22 motivators as a checklist:

- **LF8:** survival/life extension; food & drink; freedom from fear/pain/danger; sexual companionship; comfort; superiority/winning; care/protection of loved ones; social approval.
- **Learned wants:** informed; curious; clean; efficient; convenient; dependable/quality; beauty/style; economy/profit; bargains.
- **Bly's 22:** be liked, appreciated, right, important; make money; save money; save time; make work easier; secure; attractive; sexy; comfortable; distinctive; happy; have fun; gain knowledge; healthy; gratify curiosity; convenience; fear; greed; guilt.
- **Means–end chain:** ask "and what's the benefit of that?" until you reach the emotional end-state.

## 4. Voice-of-customer workflow with AI

1. **Collect** raw text (reviews, calls, tickets, forum threads). Remove personal data per privacy rules.
2. **Code** with Claude: themes, pains, desires, objections, trigger events, words/phrases, metaphors. Request *verbatim quotes with source IDs*, not paraphrases.
3. **Quantify** frequency (count mentions; don't let the model invent percentages).
4. **Human review:** sample 20 raw items against the model's summary; correct drift.
5. **Output:** BFD paragraph, language bank, objection list (ranked), proof gaps, 3–5 candidate mass desires.
6. **Mark unknowns.** Claude must list assumptions and what data would confirm them.

## 5. What to do when research is thin
- Say so. Don't fill gaps with plausible-sounding specifics.
- Run quick primary research: 5–10 customer interviews, 10 competitor teardowns, a one-question survey, search-query review, sales-team debrief.
- Draft *hypothesis copy* labelled as such and design the first test to learn the missing fact.

## 6. Research output (hand-off to next step)
