# Psychology, Desire and Persuasion Mechanics

Sources: Whitman *Cashvertising* (LF8, 17 principles, 41 techniques), Schwartz (mass desire, identification, belief), Caples Ch. 6 (appeals), Bly (22 motivators, three levels), Halbert (turning points, believability), Hopkins Ch. 6 (psychology), Cialdini (as presented by Whitman). Evidence quality: classic advertising anecdotes and academic findings of varying strength — treat as hypotheses to test.

## 1. Desire framework
- **Mass desire** can't be created, only channelled (Schwartz). Choose the strongest in urgency, staying power and scope.
- **LF8** (survival; food & drink; freedom from fear/pain/danger; companionship/sex; comfort; superiority; protecting loved ones; social approval) plus **learned wants** (informed, curious, clean, efficient, convenient, dependable, beautiful/stylish, economical, bargain).
- **Caples's classic appeals:** love/sex, greed/gain, fear, duty/honour; plus make money, save money, security, health, prestige, enjoyment, easier chores, leisure, comfort, freedom from worry, belonging, bargains, popularity, outshine neighbours (often implied).
- **Three levels (Bly):** intellectual < emotional < personal (talk to their actual situation).
- **Tension → Desire → Action** (Whitman). Reading about others fulfilling desires gives vicarious satisfaction — hence stories/case studies.
- **Means–end chain:** sell the benefit of the benefit.

## 3. Mental movies & specificity
- Replace abstract verbs/nouns with sensory, concrete ones ("pull the freshly baked pizza from the oven" vs "get something").
- Use **powerful visual adjectives**; avoid vague superlatives.
- "The first use of any product is inside the consumer's head." Rehearse the result vividly (Schwartz intensification; Halbert word pictures).

## 4. Attention & first impression
- Decision is largely made at first glance (Halbert); the layout should look **inviting/editorial**, not like an ad; relevant imagery; faces looking at the viewer (Guillotine); upbeat imagery; people like seeing people like themselves.
- **A-pile vs B-pile:** things that look personal get opened; things that look like pitches get discarded. Translate to subject/sender/preview text, ad creative that looks native — without deception.
- **Relevant attention only:** a gimmick must connect to the message (sand → Maui; peso → inflation).

## 5. Trust mechanisms
Guarantees and risk reversal · specific numbers · customer language · testimonials with specifics · third-party validation · demonstrations · transparent limits ("damaging admission") · consistent personality · human face/name · responsiveness.

## 6. Action mechanics (Kennedy/Whitman/Caples)
Overcome inertia: make it easy; ask directly; deadline (true); premium for quick action; reminders; shorten distance; "bill me later"; pre-filled forms; free trial; human assistance.

## 7. Pricing psychology (use cautiously)
People judge value by price cues (Hopkins); reference points; bundle stack; amortisation; rounding effects; "less than a coffee a day" frames. Don't mislead on total cost.

## 8. Ethics guardrails (psychology is not a licence)
- Don't exploit grief, illness, financial desperation, children, or cognitive vulnerability.
- Don't invent social proof or authority.
- Don't misrepresent urgency or scarcity.
- Respect autonomy: give enough information for a good decision; make cancelling as easy as buying.
- When in doubt, apply the **"newspaper test"**: would you be comfortable if the persuasion technique and your intent were printed beside the ad?
