# Funnels, Sequences and Campaign Architecture

Sources: Brunson (*DotCom Secrets*: Secret Formula, Value Ladder, three kinds of traffic, Soap Opera/Seinfeld, seven phases, bait test, seven funnels; *Expert Secrets*: Perfect Webinar, Epiphany funnels), Kennedy (sequence secret), Halbert (staged mailings, list hierarchy), Hopkins (samples, test campaigns), Bly (Organic/Agora model, house list, Waterfront), Whitman (TTM).

Treat funnel advice as **architecture patterns**, not magic. The principles are old (multi-step direct response); the tools are new.

For finding and reaching the audience (Dream 100, paid/retargeting, cold bridge) see `traffic-and-audience-acquisition.md`; for opt-in and lead-magnet patterns see its sections on lead magnets and capture.

## 1. The campaign map

```
WHO (dream customer)  →  WHERE (traffic sources)  →  BAIT (entry offer / lead magnet)
       →  PRE-FRAME (qualify + prepare)  →  FIRST SALE (low-risk)  →  ASCEND (core / premium)
       →  RETAIN (continuity) → REFER
```
Each step has: audience state, message, offer, success metric, next step.

## 2. Traffic types
- **Owned** (email list, SMS with consent, community): sell repeatedly at no extra media cost.
- **Controlled** (paid): you choose where it lands; buy attention and convert to owned.
- **Uncontrolled** (organic social, SEO, PR, partners): earn attention, convert to owned/controlled.
Goal: convert uncontrolled/controlled traffic into owned audience quickly (with permission and value).

## 3. Traffic temperature (Brunson) ↔ awareness (Schwartz)
| Temperature | Awareness fit | Funnel start |
|---|---|---|
| **Hot** (own customers/subscribers) | Most/Product aware | Direct offer; upsell |
| **Warm** (know you / similar) | Product / solution aware | Short bridge + offer |
| **Cold** (strangers) | Problem / unaware | Pre-frame bridge (content, quiz, story) → bait |

## 4. Seven phases (Brunson), mapped
1. Determine traffic temperature · 2. Set the pre-frame bridge · 3. Qualify subscribers (opt-in) · 4. Qualify buyers (first purchase) · 5. Identify hyperactive buyers · 6. Age and ascend (follow-up) · 7. Change the selling environment (webinar/call/event/high-touch).

## 5. Value ladder design checklist
- Entry offer solves a *specific* small problem and demonstrates your mechanism.
- Each rung delivers a standalone result.
- Price jumps match trust and value jumps.
- Include a recurring or repeat-purchase rung where natural.
- Define the "best thing you can do for them" (the top of the ladder) first, then work down.
- Map what each rung unlocks and the objection it resolves.

## 6. Pre-frame devices
Quiz/assessment · article/report · news-hook page · expert interview · case-study page · demo · calculator. Rule: the pre-frame must be genuinely useful, accurately labelled, and consistent with the offer (don't disguise ads as independent reporting).

## 7. Sequences (Kennedy + Brunson + modern email)
| Sequence | Touches | Purpose | Structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Welcome / Soap Opera** | 5 over ~5–7 days | Bond and pre-sell | Stage → drama/backstory → epiphany → hidden benefits → urgency/CTA |
| **Offer sequence** | 3–5 over 1–2 weeks | Convert interest | Offer → proof/story → objection killer → bonus/guarantee → last chance (real deadline) |
| **Nurture / Seinfeld-style** | Weekly/daily by segment | Keep warm, entertain, bridge to offers | 80–90% value/story, 10–20% bridge |
| **Cart/lead follow-up** | 2–4 within 72 hours | Recover | Reminder → help/FAQ → incentive (if sustainable) → close |
| **Win-back** | 3 over weeks | Reactivate | "Why you left" → what's changed → incentive → sunset |
| **Post-purchase / onboarding** | 3–6 | Reduce refunds, increase usage, seed referral | Welcome → quick win → social proof → upsell/referral |

**Rules**
- Each email has **one purpose, one action**; subject lines differ in angle.
- Open loops must close.
- Frequency tests by segment; honour unsubscribe immediately.
- **Sequences beat one-shots** (Kennedy) — but only if each touch adds new information or a new reason.

## 8. Webinar / live presentation (Perfect Webinar skeleton)
Intro + One Thing (the new opportunity) → three belief-breaking stories (Vehicle/Internal/External) → the stack → close with trial closes → Q&A → replay → follow-ups. Run it live repeatedly; edit from questions/objections; then automate. **NT additions:** disclose any recorded/automated format honestly; only real attendance numbers; real deadlines.

## 9. High-ticket application funnel
Pre-frame content → application form (qualify) → short VSL or call booking → discovery call script that uncovers desire/objections → offer. Keep qualification honest; don't promise outcomes you can't substantiate; keep recordings/consent rules.

## 10. Local & small-business adaptation
Value ladder example (dentist): free/low-cost check → whitening → retainers → cosmetic → recall plan. Local services can use: free estimate, guarantee, seasonal offer, referral rewards, reviews, direct mail/postcards, Google Business Profile, WhatsApp/SMS with consent.

## 11. Metrics by stage
Traffic cost · opt-in rate · lead quality · first-purchase rate · revenue per lead · payback · ascension rate · refund rate · recurring retention · referrals · overall customer acquisition cost (CAC) and lifetime value (LTV).

## 12. Common failures
- Funnel without a real ladder (no next rung).
- Pre-frame that doesn't match the offer's promise.
- Over-engineering before validating demand.
- Discounts creating low-quality buyers.
- Ignoring ops (fulfilment, support) that deliver the promise.
- Dark patterns: hidden continuity, fake countdowns, confusing cancellations.
