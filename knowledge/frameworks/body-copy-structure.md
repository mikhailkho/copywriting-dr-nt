# Body Copy: Structure, Proof, Objections, Close

Sources: Hopkins (full story, reason why), Halbert Ch. 15–19 (AIDA, closing, first impression), Kennedy Steps 7–14 (PAS, fortune-telling, winners/losers, objections, repetition, P.S., double readership path), Bly Ch. 4 (motivating sequence, 4 Ps, Copy Length Grid), Schwartz Part 2 (seven techniques), Whitman (checklist, PVAs, mental movies), Caples Ch. 11 (20 ways), Masterson/Forde (Rule of One, lead → body).

## 1. Choose a skeleton (pick one, don't mix three)

| Skeleton | Beats | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **AIDA** (Halbert/Bly) | Attention → Interest (facts) → Desire (benefits, vivid) → Action (specific, urgent) | Quick sales letters, ads |
| **Motivating sequence** (Bly) | Attention → Show a need → Satisfy the need → Prove → Ask for action | Problem-aware audiences |
| **PAS** (Kennedy) | Problem (agree) → Agitate (emotion) → Solution | Problem-aware, when risk is real |
| **4 Ps** | Picture → Promise → Prove → Push | Visual/story-led |
| **Fortune-telling** | Predict a danger/opportunity → stakes → what to do | Trend-driven/timely offers |
| **Winners & losers** | Contrast two paths → what separates them → join the winners | Aspirational/proof-heavy |
| **Epiphany Bridge** (Brunson) | Backstory → Desire → Wall → Epiphany → Plan → Conflict → Achievement → Transformation | Origin stories, webinars, long emails |
| **Schwartz flow** | Headline → desire intensification → identification → belief-building (gradualization) → mechanism → objections → proof → close | Complex persuasion for sceptical markets |
| **Idea-led (Masterson/Forde)** | Lead (one idea/emotion) → claims & benefits → proof → offer → close, all in service of one idea | Long-form direct response |

## 2. The "reader's silent questions" (Schwartz, Kennedy)
At every point the reader asks: *Tell me more. Who says so? How does it work? What's the catch? What if I'm wrong? What do I do next?* Place the answer where the question arises. Mark the **anticipation points** in your outline.

## 3. Components, in the order readers need them
1. **Hook/Lead** — see `headlines-and-leads.md`.
2. **Desire intensification** — concrete scenes of the result ("script the prospect's dreams"); sensory words; time-stretched benefits; show others enjoying it. Use **PVAs** (powerful visual adjectives) and **mental movies** (Whitman): specific, sensory, action-based.
3. **Identification** — show the roles/identity the reader wants (Schwartz); use "people like you".
4. **Credibility/belief-building** — start from beliefs they hold; move step by step (gradualization); use authority, credentials, track record, demonstration, third-party proof.
5. **Mechanism** — explain how it works in selling language (never lecture).
6. **Proof stack** — numbers, named case studies, testimonials with specifics, before/after (where truthful), demos, guarantees, press, certifications, data. Present proof **close to the claim it supports**.
7. **Objections** — list every reason not to respond; answer each with (a) a direct answer, (b) proof, (c) a guarantee reminder (Kennedy). Include real drawbacks ("damaging admission": who it's *not* for) — it raises credibility.
8. **Offer** — see `offer-construction.md`.
9. **Close** — specific instructions ("click the button below, enter X, you'll receive Y within Z"), what they gain if they act, what they lose if they delay (true), the guarantee, and ease-of-action. Halbert sometimes spent 25%+ of the copy on the close.
10. **P.S.** — second headline: restates offer/promise/deadline; skimmers read it first.

## 4. Length decision (Copy Length Grid + modifiers)
- **Emotion × involvement:** high/high (engagement ring, financial planning, coaching) → long; low/low (paper clips) → short.
- **Price** ↑ → more copy. **One-step sale** → long; **two-step lead** → shorter. **Busy audience** → front-load, shorter; **hobbyist/enthusiast** → long. **Unfamiliar product** → longer; **familiar** → shorter.
- **Write for the interested minority**, not the skimmers (Kennedy). Length itself is not the problem; boredom is (Ogilvy & Mather research cited by Kennedy: readership drops steeply to ~50 words, little from 50–500).
- Always make the first screen self-sufficient; make the rest skimmable.

## 5. Double readership path (Kennedy)
Design for two readers in one piece:
- **Impulsive path:** headline, subheads, bullets, captions, call-out boxes, bold/underline, P.S. — enough to decide.
- **Analytical path:** full facts, statistics, comparisons, FAQs.
Test by reading **only** the headings/bullets: does the offer still make sense?

## 6. Readability rules
- Average **sentence length ~6–16 words**; vary rhythm (fragments allowed).
- Short paragraphs (3–4 sentences max); white space; wide margins/line length discipline.
- Simple words; transitions ("Now…", "Here's the thing…", "Onward."); occasional question-and-answer.
- Second person, present tense; "you, you, you".
- **Eye relief:** bullets, numbering, subheads, parentheses (sparingly), photos with captions.
- **Yes-sequence:** questions the reader can only answer yes to build momentum.
- **Internal repetition:** state the core promise 5–7 ways (statement, example, story, testimonial, quote, summary).
- **Never end a page/screen section on a completed thought** if you can create a bridge ("But here's the part nobody mentions…").

## 7. Techniques for credibility and belief
- **Exact numbers:** "77.6%" > "most".
- **Proof before claim** (pre-frame belief) when the claim is extreme.
- **Demonstration:** show it working; let them test (money-back, trial).
- **Comparison:** benefit-for-benefit vs the alternative.
- **Credibility copy:** years in business, customers served, reviews, awards — specific and verifiable.
- **Two-sided messages:** acknowledge a limitation and answer it.
- **Inoculation (Whitman):** raise a likely competitor attack or objection first and answer it.
- **Authority and social proof:** named, relevant, verifiable sources.
- **Borrowed belief (Schwartz "camouflage"):** use credible formats (news-like report, expert interview) — **only if clearly labelled as advertising where required**.

## 8. Style notes
- **Personality:** a consistent voice/persona (Hopkins; Brunson's Attractive Character) builds familiarity.
- **Passion without hype:** enthusiasm; avoid "cold fish" copy (Kennedy) and avoid ALL-CAPS shouting.
- **Humour:** rarely; if used, in service of the point.
- **Don't help competitors:** avoid generic category claims ("fast, reliable service") that any rival could use.
- **Know when to stop:** once the reader has what they need, close.

## 9. Draft → edit loop
1. **First draft long**, without editing; get every persuasive idea down (Kennedy: 2–3× final length).
2. **Strategic rewrite:** structure, order, proof placement, objections.
3. **Style rewrite:** voice, rhythm, specificity.
4. **Passion pass:** where does it sound flat? where is the emotion?
5. **Clarity edit:** cut every word that doesn't sell; read aloud.
6. **Cool-off (≥ a night)**, second opinions from *target-market* readers.
7. **Final checks:** facts, claims, links, legal.

## 10. Output template (long-form piece)
```
HEADLINE / SUBHEAD
LEAD (archetype: __)
DESIRE SCENES (2–3)
BELIEF BUILD + MECHANISM
PROOF STACK
OBJECTION HANDLING (top 5)
OFFER (stack, price, guarantee, bonus, reason why)
CLOSE (instructions + gain/loss)
P.S.
```
